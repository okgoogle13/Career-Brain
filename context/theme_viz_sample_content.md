# Key Selection Criteria

**Application:** Program Coordinator — Riverside Community Health

## Criterion 1: Demonstrated experience coordinating community programs with multiple stakeholders

**Context:** At Hope Community Network I coordinate six concurrent programs serving about 1,200
clients a year. When I started, referrals from three partner agencies arrived by email, phone and fax,
and nobody tracked whether clients reached a first appointment. Average wait time was 14 days, and
partners had raised the delays at two consecutive quarterly meetings.

**Action:** I met each partner's intake lead to map how referrals moved between services and where
they stalled. We agreed a single referral form and a weekly 30-minute triage meeting. I set up a
shared spreadsheet tracking each referral from receipt to first appointment, with a named owner for
every step. I reported conversion and wait-time figures to partners each month and raised stalled
referrals at the triage meeting rather than by email. When one agency's referrals kept stalling, I
arranged a joint session with their team to find out why and adjusted the form to collect the
consent details they had been missing.

**Result:** Within nine months average wait time fell from 14 to 5 days and referral conversion rose
from 41% to 69%. The partners adopted the form permanently, and the figures were used in two
successful funding renewals. A fourth agency asked to join the triage meeting the following year.

- Chaired 40 weekly triage meetings over the first year.
- Trained 6 partner staff on the shared referral form.

## Criterion 2: Ability to supervise and support staff in a trauma-informed practice environment

**Context:** At Southside Housing Services I acted as team leader for seven months, supervising eight
case managers working with clients with complex needs, including family violence, homelessness and
co-occurring mental health concerns. Three staff had left in the previous six months, supervision was
irregular, and incident debriefs depended on who was on shift.

**Action:** I set up fortnightly one-to-one supervision for every case manager, using a structured
agenda covering caseload, wellbeing and professional development. I introduced a standard debrief
within 48 hours of any critical incident, focused on what the team could change rather than on fault.
I adjusted caseload allocation so that no worker held more than three clients assessed as high risk,
and reviewed the allocation each fortnight with the program manager. When a worker showed signs of
vicarious trauma, I arranged access to the employee assistance program, reduced their high-risk
cases for a month and checked in weekly. I also started a monthly team reflective practice session
where staff brought one case to discuss using the organisation's trauma-informed practice framework.

**Result:** No case manager left during the seven months, compared with three departures in the
previous six. All critical incidents were debriefed within 48 hours, and two practice changes from
those debriefs were added to the team's procedures. The reflective practice session continued after
the permanent team leader started.

- Delivered 110 supervision sessions across the acting period.
- Wrote the incident debrief template still used by the team.
